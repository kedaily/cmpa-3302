# Foundational Innovators

A small number of individuals turned laboratory curiosities into a new form of entertainment and a new industry.

## Laboratory Pioneers

**William Higinbotham**  
Created *Tennis for Two* (1958) at Brookhaven National Laboratory. He built the game to make an open house more engaging and was surprised by its popularity.

**Steve Russell**  
Lead developer of *Spacewar!* (1962) at MIT. The game’s free distribution across university computers spread the concept of interactive digital play.

## Console and Arcade Pioneers

**Ralph Baer**  
Often called the Father of Video Games. He led development of the Brown Box prototype at Sanders Associates, which became the Magnavox Odyssey (1972). His patents covered core home video game technology.

**Nolan Bushnell**  
Co-founder of Atari. He co-created *Computer Space* and later guided the company that released *Pong*. He recognized the commercial potential of video games earlier than most.

**Ted Dabney**  
Bushnell’s early collaborator on *Computer Space* and co-founder of Atari. He left the company relatively early but was central to its initial technical work.

**Allan Alcorn**  
Atari engineer who designed *Pong*. What began as a training exercise became the first widely successful commercial video game.

## How Their Work Connects

```
Laboratory games (Higinbotham, Russell)
		↓
Baer’s home console concept
		↓
Bushnell sees Odyssey → inspires Pong (Alcorn)
		↓
Commercial industry begins
```
