# Home Consoles of the 1970s

The Magnavox Odyssey proved that a dedicated game system could work in the home. The rest of the decade saw rapid experimentation with how home video games should be packaged and sold. 

## The Odyssey Model

The Odyssey (1972) used built-in games and screen overlays. It sold modestly, about 200,000 units, but established the category of "home video game console."

## Dedicated Consoles

After *Pong*'s arcade success, many companies released single-purpose home systems that played only tennis-style games. These dedicated consoles were inexpensive and easy to yse, but they could not be expanded with new games. The market quickly became crowded with similar products. 

## The Shift to Cartridges: Atari 2600 (1977)

Atari released the Video Computer System (later renamed the Atari 2600) in 1977. It introduced a new model:

- Interchangeable game cartridges
- Joystick and paddle controllers
- Color graphics
- A growing library of titles from both Atari and third-party developers

The 2600 is widely regarded as the beginning of the second generation of home consoles and the end of the pure "early era."
