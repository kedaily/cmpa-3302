# Pre-Commercial Experiments (1947-1970)

Video games began as technical demonstrations rather than entertainment products. Researchers and engineers created interactive displays to test computers, entertain visitors, or explore new possibilities. 

## Earliest Interactive Devices 

### Cathode-ray Tube Amusement Devices (1947)

Thomas T. Goldsmith Jr and Estle Ray Mann filed a patent for a device that let players control a CRT beam to "hit" targets. It was the first known interactive electronic game, through it was never produced commercially. 

### OXO (1952)

A.S. Douglas programmed a tic-tac-toe game called OXO on the EDSAC computer at Cambridge University. It ran as part of his research into human-computer interaction and is frequently cited among the earliest video games. 

## Public Demonstrations

### Tennis for Two (1958)

William Higinbotham created Tennis for Two for Brookhaven National Laboratory's visitors' day. Two players controlled a bouncing dot on an oscilloscope. The game proved so popular that people waited in long lines to try it. 

Higinbotham later noted that he built the game simply to make the laboratory's open house more interesting. 

### Spacewar! (1962)

Steve Russell and fellow MIT students developed Spacewar! on a PDP-1 computer. Two players controlled spaceships that battled around a start with realistic gravity. Because the code was freely shared. Spacewar! spread to other universities and became the first widely distributed video game. 

## Characteristics of this Period

Games existed only on expensive research computers or custom hardware.
No commercial distribution or business model.
Primary audiences were scientists, students, and laboratory visitors.

Focus was on proving technical concepts rather than entertainment value. These experiments established that real-time interactive graphics were possible and directly inspired the commercial pioneers who followed.
