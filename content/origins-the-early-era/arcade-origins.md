# Arcade Origins

Coin-operated video games turned interactive experiments into a public entertainment business.

## First Commercial Cabinets

### Computer Space (1971)

The first video game sold in arcades. Inspired by *Spacewars!*, it featured two-player space combat. Its complexity made it less accessible than traditional arcade machines such as pinball.

### Galaxy Game (1971)

Installed at Stanford University, this *Spacewar!* adaptation was coin-operated but only one machine was ever built. 

## The *Pong* Explosion (1972)

*Pong* succeeded because it was simple, social, and instantly understandable. Players needed only one control each. The game's success led Atari to manufacture thousands of cabinets and inspired dozens of clones. 

**Notable details**

- First major public location: Andy Capp's Tavern in Sunnyvale, California.
- Coin box filled so fast the machine stopped working. 
- Sold over 8,000 units in its early run.

## From Novelty to Industry 

By the mid-to-late 1970s, arcades had become established venues. Games such as *Space Invaders* (1978) would soon drive the golden age, but the foundation was laid by *Computer Space* and especially *Pong*.

### Key Differences from Home Systems

- Pay-per-play model
- Designed for short, competitive sessions
- Placed in bars, arcades, and public spaces
- Generated immediate revenue without requiring a large upfront purchase
